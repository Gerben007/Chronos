Moses is the great deliverer and law-giver of the Old Testament:
prophet, judge, intercessor, the man with whom God spoke "face to
face, as a man speaketh unto his friend" (Exodus 33:11).

## Born under judgement

Born to a Levite family in Egypt during Pharaoh's edict that every
Hebrew baby boy be killed (Exodus 1:22 – 2:10), Moses was preserved
in an ark of bulrushes and raised in Pharaoh's own household.
Hebrews 11:24-26 records his choice as a young man: rather than
"enjoy the pleasures of sin for a season", he "esteem[ed] the
reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures in Egypt".

## The burning bush

After forty years in the wilderness of Midian (Acts 7:30), Moses
encountered the LORD in the burning bush (Exodus 3). God revealed
His covenant name — **"I AM THAT I AM"** — and commissioned Moses to
lead Israel out of bondage.

## The ten plagues

The plagues recorded in Exodus 7-12 were not random afflictions but
targeted judgements against the gods of Egypt (Exodus 12:12). The
final plague — the death of every firstborn — was averted only for
those who had marked their doorposts with the blood of a lamb.
This first **Passover** anchors the entire redemptive structure of
Scripture; Paul calls Christ "our passover" (1 Corinthians 5:7).

## Red Sea, Sinai, wilderness

Israel went out by the strong hand of the LORD (Exodus 14), crossed
the Red Sea on dry ground, and received the Law at Mount Sinai
(Exodus 19-20). The remainder of the Pentateuch records forty years
of wilderness wandering and the establishment of the tabernacle, the
priesthood, and the sacrificial system — all "a shadow of good
things to come" (Hebrews 10:1).

## Dates

Ussher's chronology places the Exodus c. **1491 BC**. Mainstream
conservative scholars (e.g. Kitchen) date it to **1446 BC** based on
1 Kings 6:1. The "late-date" view (c. 1280 BC, Ramesses II) is
common in more critical scholarship but cannot be reconciled with
the biblical 480-year datum.
