The Old Kingdom (Dynasties 3 to 6) is the era in which the
characteristic features of pharaonic Egypt — a centralised state, a
divine king, mastery of large-scale stone architecture — emerged in
fully developed form. It is sometimes called the Pyramid Age.

## Memphis the capital

Founded near the apex of the Nile delta, Memphis served as Egypt's
administrative and religious centre throughout the Old Kingdom.
Just outside the city, the necropolis of Saqqara was developed
into the burial precinct of the kings.

## The great pyramids

- **Djoser** (Dynasty 3, c. 2670 BC) — Step Pyramid at Saqqara,
  designed by Imhotep. The earliest monumental stone building in
  the world.
- **Sneferu** (early Dynasty 4) — Bent Pyramid and Red Pyramid at
  Dahshur, refining the geometry that would peak under his son.
- **Khufu** (Dynasty 4, c. 2580 BC) — Great Pyramid of Giza, the
  largest of the three.
- **Khafre** and **Menkaure** — second and third Giza pyramids;
  the Sphinx is from Khafre's reign.

The labour required was vast but not, as ancient writers imagined,
slave labour. Excavation of the workmen's village at Giza shows
salaried teams of free Egyptians on rotation.

## Religion and writing

The Old Kingdom is the period in which **Pyramid Texts** — the
earliest known religious literature — were carved on the walls of
the chambers of late Dynasty-5 and Dynasty-6 pyramids. The texts
guide the king's spirit into the afterlife. Hieroglyphic writing
was already mature when the Old Kingdom began.

## Collapse

The Old Kingdom ended c. 2181 BC in what Egyptologists call the
**First Intermediate Period** — a century or two of fragmented
authority, climate stress, and competing local rulers. The fault
lines were a steady decentralisation of power to provincial nomarchs
and a series of low Nile floods.

## Biblical position

Old-Kingdom Egypt is roughly contemporary with the early
post-flood patriarchs in Ussher's chronology — Peleg, Reu, Serug.
Abraham's visit to Egypt (Genesis 12) falls late in the First
Intermediate Period or early Middle Kingdom, depending on which
king list dates one adopts.
