Joseph, the eleventh son of Jacob and the firstborn of Rachel, is one
of the most fully-drawn figures of Genesis. His story occupies more
chapters than any other patriarch's — 14 of 50 — and bridges the
patriarchal narratives to the Exodus.

## Sold by his brothers

Singled out by his father's favour (the coat of many colours,
Genesis 37:3), and given prophetic dreams of his future authority,
Joseph was hated by his brothers. They sold him to a passing
caravan of Ishmaelites; he was carried down to Egypt and sold again
into the house of Potiphar, captain of Pharaoh's guard.

## Imprisoned, exalted

Falsely accused by Potiphar's wife, Joseph was thrown into the royal
prison, where God gave him understanding to interpret the dreams of
the king's butler and baker (Genesis 40). Two years later, when
Pharaoh dreamed of seven fat and seven lean cattle, Joseph was
brought from the dungeon — and after interpreting the dream as
seven years of plenty followed by seven of famine, was set over all
Egypt at the age of thirty (Genesis 41:46).

## Reunion and forgiveness

When the famine came, Jacob's other sons travelled to Egypt for
grain. Joseph recognised them at once; they did not recognise him.
After testing them severely (Genesis 42-44), Joseph revealed himself
in one of Scripture's most affecting scenes — and brought his
father and seventy souls down to dwell in Goshen.

His summary verdict on the whole arc has shaped Christian theology
of providence ever since:

> But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto
> good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people
> alive. (Genesis 50:20, KJV)

## Significance

- The descent to Egypt sets the stage for the four hundred years of
  bondage and the Exodus.
- Hebrews 11:22 commends Joseph's faith especially for his dying
  request that his bones be carried up to the promised land — a
  testimony that he believed God's covenant with Abraham would be
  kept.
- Joseph's life — beloved son, sold for silver, falsely accused,
  exalted at the right hand of the king, saviour of his people —
  is one of the clearest Old-Testament types of Christ.
