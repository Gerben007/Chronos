David, the youngest son of Jesse of Bethlehem, is **the man after
God's own heart** (1 Samuel 13:14; Acts 13:22): shepherd, warrior,
king, and psalmist. With him the line of promise narrows further —
the Messiah will be the son of David, born in David's city
(Matthew 1:1; Luke 2:11).

## Anointed in secret

Saul, Israel's first king, had been rejected for disobedience.
Samuel was sent to Bethlehem to anoint a new king from among Jesse's
sons. Seven sons passed before him; the LORD chose none of them.
"The LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward
appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart" (1 Samuel 16:7).
David, in the field with the sheep, was the eighth, and the chosen.

## Goliath, Saul, the wilderness years

Still a youth, David faced the Philistine champion Goliath with a
sling and five smooth stones (1 Samuel 17) — "the battle is the
LORD's". His subsequent rise in Saul's court provoked the king's
jealousy and forced him into years of exile in the wilderness. The
psalms born in those years (e.g. Psalm 57, written "when he fled
from Saul in the cave") are some of the most pastorally rich in
Scripture.

## King over Judah, then all Israel

After Saul's death at Gilboa, David reigned seven years over Judah
at Hebron, then over all twelve tribes from Jerusalem, the city he
took from the Jebusites and made the capital (2 Samuel 5).

## The Davidic covenant

When David proposed to build a temple, the LORD answered through
Nathan with a greater promise: **God would build David a house** —
a dynasty whose throne would be established forever (2 Samuel 7:12-16).
This covenant is the seedbed of every messianic prophecy that
follows; the New Testament begins by tracing Jesus back to it
(Matthew 1:1).

## Failure and repentance

David's adultery with Bathsheba and the murder of Uriah (2 Samuel 11)
is not glossed over by Scripture. The judgement was severe; the
repentance was real (Psalm 51). David is great not because he was
sinless but because, when confronted, he repented without excuse —
a model the rest of the kings of Israel and Judah rarely matched.

## Psalms

Roughly half the canonical Psalter is attributed to David. The Spirit
of God spoke through him (2 Samuel 23:2; Acts 1:16), and the
prophetic depth of his psalms — Psalm 22, Psalm 110, and others —
reaches forward to Christ.
