By the days of Noah the wickedness of man on earth had become so
great that "every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only
evil continually" (Genesis 6:5). God resolved to send a flood, but
Noah "found grace in the eyes of the LORD" (Genesis 6:8).

## The ark

God instructed Noah to build an ark of gopher wood, 300 cubits long,
50 wide, 30 high (Genesis 6:14-16). Hebrews 11:7 commends Noah's
obedience: he built the ark "by faith", on God's word alone, before
any rain had fallen.

Into the ark went Noah, his wife, his three sons (Shem, Ham, and
Japheth) and their wives — eight souls in all (1 Peter 3:20) — along
with the animals God brought to him.

## The flood

The waters prevailed for 150 days; every living thing on the dry
land died (Genesis 7:21-24). The ark came to rest on the mountains
of Ararat. When the waters had abated, Noah built an altar and
offered burnt offerings.

## The covenant of the rainbow

God's response was a covenant: never again would He destroy the
earth by water (Genesis 9:11). The rainbow was set as the sign of
this promise — a sign that endures every time it appears.

## Significance

Peter draws two lines from the flood:

- It is a real historical judgement, demonstrating that God will
  not let unrepented sin stand (2 Peter 2:5).
- It prefigures the salvation of the church through Christ — as the
  ark bore Noah safely through judgement, so baptism, "the answer of
  a good conscience toward God," now saves us through the
  resurrection of Jesus (1 Peter 3:21).
