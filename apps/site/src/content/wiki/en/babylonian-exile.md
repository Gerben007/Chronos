The Babylonian Exile was the deportation of Judah from the
promised land to Babylon by Nebuchadnezzar II. It came in waves —
**605 BC, 597 BC, and 586 BC** — and lasted seventy years, exactly
as the LORD had foretold through Jeremiah (Jeremiah 25:11-12).

## Three deportations

- **605 BC** — Nebuchadnezzar took Jerusalem after defeating Egypt
  at Carchemish. Among the captives were members of the royal house,
  including Daniel and his three friends (Daniel 1:1-7).
- **597 BC** — A second deportation followed Jehoiachin's brief
  reign. Ezekiel was carried away in this group.
- **586 BC** — After Zedekiah's revolt, Nebuchadnezzar's army razed
  Jerusalem and destroyed the temple. The vessels of the house of
  God were carried to Babylon (2 Kings 25:8-21).

## Theology of exile

The exile was understood, both by the prophets and by the exiles
themselves, as **the LORD's discipline** for centuries of covenant
unfaithfulness — chiefly the idolatry that had taken root in both
Israel and Judah. Daniel's prayer of confession (Daniel 9:4-19)
and the lament of Psalm 137 give the inner shape of the period.

Yet exile was not abandonment:

- Jeremiah wrote to the exiles to "build ye houses, and dwell in
  them" and to "seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you
  to be carried away captives" (Jeremiah 29:5,7). They were to live
  faithfully where they were placed.
- Ezekiel ministered among them by the river Chebar, and saw the
  glory of the LORD depart from Jerusalem and (in chapter 43) return
  to a future temple.
- Daniel served in the Babylonian and Persian courts, witnessing to
  Nebuchadnezzar, Belshazzar, and Darius, and receiving the visions
  of empires to come.

## Return

In 538 BC, the first year of Cyrus the Persian (who had taken
Babylon the previous year), the LORD stirred up his spirit to issue
a decree: "all his kingdom" was to permit the Jews to return and
rebuild the temple (Ezra 1:1-4). Isaiah had named Cyrus a century
and a half earlier (Isaiah 44:28 - 45:1) — one of the most
celebrated specific prophecies of the Old Testament.

The return was led by Zerubbabel and Joshua the high priest, with
about 50,000 exiles. The second temple was completed in 516 BC,
exactly seventy years after the destruction of the first.

## Significance

After the exile, idolatry largely disappeared from Judah. The
post-exilic community produced the synagogue, the standardised
Hebrew text, and the deep messianic expectation that the New
Testament inherits and answers in Christ.
