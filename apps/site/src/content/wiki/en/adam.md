Adam is the first man, created by God on the sixth day of creation
(Genesis 1:26-31). Formed from the dust of the ground, he was placed
in the Garden of Eden and given dominion over the earth.

## A federal head

Scripture treats Adam not only as the biological ancestor of mankind
but as our **representative head**. His disobedience in eating from
the tree of the knowledge of good and evil (Genesis 3) brought sin
and death upon the whole human race (Romans 5:12-19). Christ, the
"last Adam" (1 Corinthians 15:45), reverses what the first Adam
brought, restoring those united to Him to right standing with God.

## Eden, the Fall, and the first promise

The fall recorded in Genesis 3 is not merely a tragic story; it is
the hinge on which the rest of Scripture turns. Three things follow
immediately:

- The serpent is cursed (Genesis 3:14-15).
- Eve and the woman's seed are placed in enmity with the serpent —
  this is the *protoevangelium*, the first promise of a Saviour.
- Adam is sent out from the garden, and the way to the tree of life
  is guarded.

The remainder of the Old Testament traces the line of promise from
Adam, through Seth, Noah, Abraham, and David, to Christ.

## Dates

Ussher places Adam's creation in **4004 BC**, with his death at
3074 BC (Genesis 5:5 — "all the days that Adam lived were nine
hundred and thirty years"). Alternate Old-Testament chronologies
(Masoretic, LXX) give different totals; Chronos surfaces these as
notes on the entry where they differ meaningfully.
