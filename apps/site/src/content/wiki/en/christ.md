Jesus Christ — eternal Son of God, born of the virgin Mary in
Bethlehem in the days of Caesar Augustus and Herod the Great — is
the centre of Scripture and the centre of history. Every line in
Chronos either leads to Him or is read in His light.

## Person

The Council of Nicaea (AD 325) and Chalcedon (AD 451) confessed what
Scripture teaches: that Christ is **fully God and fully man**, two
natures united in one Person, without confusion, without change,
without division, without separation. He is "the brightness of
[the Father's] glory, and the express image of his person"
(Hebrews 1:3) and at the same time the Word made flesh, who
"dwelt among us" (John 1:14).

## Ministry

The four Gospels record approximately three years of public
ministry. He proclaimed the kingdom of God, taught with an authority
the scribes lacked (Matthew 7:29), healed the sick, raised the
dead, forgave sins, and predicted His own death and resurrection.
His miracles were not stunts but signs (John 20:30-31) — public
evidence that He is the Christ, the Son of God.

## The cross

> For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son,
> that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have
> everlasting life. (John 3:16, KJV)

The crucifixion is the hinge of redemptive history. Christ died
**substitutionarily**: He bore the wrath due our sin so that those
who trust Him might be reckoned righteous in Him (Isaiah 53:5-6;
2 Corinthians 5:21). His death was no accident; "him, being delivered
by the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God, ye have taken,
and by wicked hands have crucified and slain" (Acts 2:23).

## The resurrection

On the third day He rose from the dead, "the firstfruits of them
that slept" (1 Corinthians 15:20). The empty tomb is a fact attested
by eyewitnesses (1 Corinthians 15:5-8), proclaimed publicly in
Jerusalem within weeks, and the foundation of Christian faith — if
Christ is not risen, our preaching is vain and our faith is also
vain (1 Corinthians 15:14).

## Ascension and present reign

Forty days after the resurrection He ascended (Acts 1:9-11), and
sits "on the right hand of the Majesty on high" (Hebrews 1:3),
making intercession for His people (Hebrews 7:25). The church now
proclaims His gospel to every nation until He returns.

## Dates

Conservative scholarship places Christ's birth at **4-6 BC** (Herod
died in 4 BC) and the crucifixion at **AD 30 or AD 33**. Chronos
uses 4 BC – AD 33 in headlines; the entry surfaces alternate dates
where they differ meaningfully.
