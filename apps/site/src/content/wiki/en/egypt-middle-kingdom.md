The Middle Kingdom (Dynasties 11 and 12) follows the disorder of the
First Intermediate Period. It is often described as Egypt's classical
age: literature, statuary, and provincial administration all reach
high points.

## Reunification

**Mentuhotep II** (Dynasty 11, c. 2055 BC) reunified the country
from his base at Thebes, ending the patchwork rule of competing
local dynasties. He moved the focus of the kingdom south, though
later Middle-Kingdom rulers operated from Itj-Tawy near Memphis.

## Dynasty 12

Founded by **Amenemhat I**, Dynasty 12 lasted about two centuries
and is the Middle Kingdom's heart. Its rulers — Senusret I, II, and
III; Amenemhat II, III, and IV — combined administrative reform,
Nubian expansion (with new forts at the second cataract), and
ambitious irrigation projects in the Fayum oasis.

## Literature

The Middle Kingdom produced the texts that later schoolboys would
copy for the next millennium:

- The *Tale of Sinuhe* — a courtier's flight to Canaan and eventual
  return to Egypt, written in beautiful classical Egyptian.
- The *Instructions* of Amenemhat, of Ptahhotep (preserved from
  earlier), of King Merikare — wisdom literature with notable
  resemblances to Proverbs in style and concern.
- *Hymn to Hapi* (the Nile flood), the *Eloquent Peasant*, and a
  series of pessimistic dialogues.

The art is austere and personal: the carved faces of Senusret III
and Amenemhat III are unflinching, almost severe, in contrast to
the idealised Old-Kingdom royal portraiture.

## Decline

Dynasty 12 ended with the brief reign of Sobekneferu, the first
attested female pharaoh. The weak Dynasty 13 followed, marked by
rapid succession of kings and the slow infiltration of Asiatic
peoples — the Hyksos — into the eastern delta. By c. 1650 BC the
country was again divided.

## Biblical position

Most conservative chronologies place **Joseph's rise to power**
within Dynasty 12 (Senusret III or Amenemhat III, c. 1880-1840 BC).
The descent of Jacob and his sons would have occurred under a
favourable Egyptian regime, with the years of bondage beginning
only after a new dynasty "which knew not Joseph" (Exodus 1:8) —
most plausibly the Hyksos or, on the early-Exodus dating, the early
New Kingdom.
