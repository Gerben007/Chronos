After Alexander the Great's conquest of the Persian Empire and his
death in 323 BC, his vast holdings were divided among his generals.
Egypt fell to **Ptolemy son of Lagos**, who founded the Ptolemaic
dynasty that ruled the country for nearly three centuries — the
last of the pharaohs, and the longest-lived of any of the
Hellenistic successor kingdoms.

## A Greek-speaking dynasty

Egypt was ruled in Greek; the Ptolemies built their capital not at
Memphis or Thebes but at **Alexandria**, the new port city founded
by Alexander. The country was governed bilingually, with hieroglyphic
and demotic Egyptian alongside Greek (the Rosetta Stone, 196 BC, is
a single decree carved in all three).

The Ptolemies presented themselves to the Egyptian priesthood as
proper pharaohs — built temples (Edfu, Dendera, Philae) in
traditional style, were crowned at Memphis — while operating a
classical Hellenistic state.

## Alexandria

Under Ptolemy I and Ptolemy II, Alexandria became the most important
intellectual centre of the ancient world:

- The **Library of Alexandria** held perhaps half a million scrolls.
- The **Mouseion**, a state-funded research institution, supported
  Euclid (Elements), Eratosthenes (measured the earth's
  circumference), Aristarchus (heliocentrism), Archimedes (visiting),
  and many others.
- Greek translation of the Hebrew Scriptures — the **Septuagint** —
  was begun here in the third century BC, traditionally under
  Ptolemy II Philadelphus.

## The Septuagint

The Septuagint (LXX) is the Greek translation used by the writers of
the New Testament; the great majority of Old-Testament quotations in
the NT follow it. Where the LXX differs in chronology from the
Masoretic Text (e.g. longer patriarchal life-spans before Abraham),
Chronos records the alternate in `start_year_alt_json`.

## Decline and end

The dynasty was beset by sibling rivalries, mob unrest in Alexandria,
and steadily increasing Roman influence. **Cleopatra VII**, the
last of the line, allied first with Julius Caesar and then with
Mark Antony; their defeat by Octavian at Actium in 31 BC ended
Ptolemaic Egypt. Octavian formally annexed Egypt in **30 BC**, and
it remained a Roman province until the Arab conquest seven
centuries later.

## Biblical position

The Ptolemaic period spans the inter-testamental centuries —
between Malachi and the birth of Christ. The Greek that the New
Testament authors wrote was the same Koine Greek the Ptolemies
spoke, and the Old Testament their first readers knew was
overwhelmingly the LXX produced in Alexandria.
