Abraham — originally **Abram**, "exalted father", later renamed by
God to **Abraham**, "father of a multitude" (Genesis 17:5) — is the
patriarch through whom the line of promise narrows from Adam toward
Christ. Called out of Ur of the Chaldees, he is the first man
Scripture names as *justified by faith* (Genesis 15:6).

## The call

> Now the LORD had said unto Abram, Get thee out of thy country, and
> from thy kindred, and from thy father's house, unto a land that I
> will shew thee: And I will make of thee a great nation, and I will
> bless thee, and make thy name great; and thou shalt be a blessing.
> (Genesis 12:1-2, KJV)

Three things were promised: a **land** (Canaan), a **seed** (an
innumerable people), and a **blessing** to all nations through him.
Paul names this last promise the gospel preached beforehand to
Abraham (Galatians 3:8).

## The covenant

The covenant is formally ratified in Genesis 15 and confirmed in
Genesis 17, where circumcision is given as the covenant sign. Hebrews
11 lists Abraham among the great roll of faith — he obeyed when
called, dwelt in tents in a foreign land, and waited for the city
"whose builder and maker is God" (Hebrews 11:10).

## Isaac on Mount Moriah

Genesis 22 records the binding of Isaac, where Abraham — at God's
command — prepares to offer his only son. The angel of the LORD stays
his hand, and a ram caught in a thicket is offered instead. The
passage looks forward in unmistakable typology to the Father who did
not spare His own Son (Romans 8:32), and to "the Lamb of God, which
taketh away the sin of the world" (John 1:29).

## Father of the faithful

Paul argues at length in Romans 4 that Abraham is the father of all
who believe — Jew and Gentile — because his righteousness was reckoned
to him not by works of the law but by faith. The promise is to "those
who are of the faith of Abraham" (Romans 4:16).
