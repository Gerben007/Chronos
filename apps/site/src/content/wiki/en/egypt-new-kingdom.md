The New Kingdom (Dynasties 18, 19, 20) is the period of Egypt's
empire — the era in which the country pushed south past the fourth
cataract of the Nile and north into the Levant as far as the
Euphrates. It is also the era the Old Testament intersects with
most directly.

## The expulsion of the Hyksos

Dynasty 17 of Thebes, under Seqenenre Tao and his sons Kamose and
Ahmose, fought a long war against the Hyksos rulers of the delta.
**Ahmose I** (c. 1550 BC) finally drove them out and reunified
Egypt — the conventional start of the New Kingdom.

## The empire under Dynasty 18

- **Thutmose I** (c. 1493 BC) campaigned south to Kurgus and east
  to the Euphrates.
- **Hatshepsut** (c. 1473-1458 BC) — Egypt's most celebrated female
  pharaoh; built the mortuary temple of Deir el-Bahri.
- **Thutmose III** — the great warrior-pharaoh; seventeen campaigns
  into the Levant; the battle of Megiddo (1457 BC) opened the road
  to Lebanon.
- **Amenhotep II, Thutmose IV, Amenhotep III** — diplomatic consolidation,
  monumental building.

## The Amarna interlude

**Akhenaten** (c. 1353-1336 BC) abolished the traditional cults,
moved the capital to a new city (Akhetaten / Amarna), and elevated
the sun-disc *Aten* as sole god. The Amarna Letters — diplomatic
correspondence on cuneiform tablets from Canaanite vassal kings —
date to his reign and provide a rich picture of Late Bronze-Age
Canaan, including the *Apiru* (often connected by scholars with
the Hebrews) raiding the hill country.

After Akhenaten's death, **Tutankhamun** restored the old religion;
the dynasty ended with Horemheb.

## Dynasty 19: Seti and Ramesses

- **Seti I** (c. 1290 BC) campaigned vigorously in the Levant.
- **Ramesses II** (c. 1279-1213 BC) — 66 years on the throne;
  fought the Hittites at Kadesh (1274 BC) to a draw, then concluded
  history's first known peace treaty. Built on a scale not seen
  since the pyramid age.
- **Merneptah** (c. 1213-1203 BC) — his victory stele is the
  earliest extra-biblical mention of **Israel** as a people in
  Canaan ("Israel is laid waste; his seed is not"), dating to
  c. 1208 BC.

## Decline

Dynasty 20 produced one more great king — Ramesses III, who
repelled the Sea Peoples in c. 1175 BC — but thereafter declined
through a series of weak Ramessides until ending c. 1077 BC.

## Biblical position

The **Exodus** falls in the New Kingdom by any conservative
chronology:

- *Early date* (1 Kings 6:1 datum): c. **1446 BC**, under Amenhotep II.
- *Late date* (architectural / cultural reading): c. 1290-1260 BC,
  under Ramesses II.

The Merneptah Stele requires that Israel be a settled entity in
Canaan by 1208 BC — a hard ceiling for either dating.
